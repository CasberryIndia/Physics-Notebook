A timeless demonstration of periodic motion, harmonic oscillation, and energy conservation.

01. Periodic Motion

Harmonic Oscillation

A simple pendulum consists of a mass suspended from a fixed point by a massless string. When displaced from equilibrium and released, it exhibits periodic motion, swinging back and forth with a constant period.

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Here, $T$ is the period, $L$ is the length of the string, and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity. Notice that the period is independent of the mass and, for small angles, independent of amplitude.

Real World Analogy

The Grandfather Clock

Pendulums have been used for centuries in clocks. The consistent period of oscillation makes them perfect timekeepers—change the length to adjust the time.

02. Energy Transfer

Conservation of Energy

As the pendulum swings, energy continuously converts between kinetic and potential energy. At the highest point of its swing, all energy is gravitational potential. At the lowest point, all energy is kinetic.

$$E_{total} = KE + PE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + mgh = \text{constant}$$

The total mechanical energy remains constant in an ideal pendulum (no friction or air resistance). This interchange demonstrates one of physics' most fundamental principles: energy conservation.

Deep Concept

Small Angle Approximation

For angles less than about 15°, we can approximate $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ (in radians). This simplification allows us to treat the pendulum as a simple harmonic oscillator with the restoring force $F = -mg\sin\theta \approx -mg\theta$.

03. Real-World Effects

Damping & Resistance

In reality, no pendulum swings forever. Air resistance and friction at the pivot point gradually reduce the amplitude of oscillation. This energy loss is called damping.

$$F_{damping} = -b\dot{\theta}$$

Here, $b$ is the damping coefficient and $\dot{\theta}$ is the angular velocity. The damping force opposes motion, causing the pendulum to eventually come to rest.

Engineering Application

Shock Absorbers

Car suspension systems use damped oscillators to absorb bumps smoothly. The damping prevents the car from bouncing indefinitely after hitting a pothole.
