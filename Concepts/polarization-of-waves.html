The geometric orientation of a wave's oscillations and its behavior as it passes through filters.

01. Linear Polarization

Oscillating in One Plane

An electromagnetic wave consists of electric and magnetic fields oscillating perpendicular to the direction of travel. In a linearly polarized wave, the electric field vector is confined to a single plane along the direction of propagation.

$$ \vec{E}(z,t) = E_0 \cos(kz - \omega t) \hat{n} $$
Real World Analogy

Shaking a Rope

Imagine shaking a rope up and down to create a wave. If you only move your hand vertically, the wave is vertically polarized. If you shake it side-to-side, it is horizontally polarized.

02. Polarization States

Circular and Elliptical

When two orthogonal linearly polarized waves (e.g., in the x and y directions) superpose, their combined state depends on their relative amplitudes and the phase difference $\delta$ between them. A phase difference of $90^\circ$ ($\pi/2$) with equal amplitudes produces circularly polarized light.

$$ E_x = E_{0x} \cos(\omega t) \quad ; \quad E_y = E_{0y} \cos(\omega t + \delta) $$
Visualizing the Trace

Lissajous Figures

Looking at the wave head-on, the tip of the electric field vector traces out a shape over time. It can be a straight line, a circle, or an ellipse, forming Lissajous figures depending on the phase and amplitude ratios.

03. Malus's Law

Passing Through Polarizers

When linearly polarized light of intensity $I_0$ passes through a polarizing filter (analyzer) whose transmission axis is at an angle $\theta$ relative to the incoming polarization, the transmitted intensity $I$ is reduced according to Malus's Law.

$$ I = I_0 \cos^2(\theta) $$
Real World Analogy

Picket Fence

If you shake a rope to make a wave and pass it through the vertical slats of a picket fence, only vertical waves get through. If you try to pass horizontal waves through the same vertical fence, they are completely blocked.
